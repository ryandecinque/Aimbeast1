Playlist: Video check of this morning's 12 Zeus Track runs (from the Twitch VOD)
How I measured it: I downloaded the Zeus part of the stream (06:10–07:00, 1600×900 at 60 fps) and matched each run to the practice log using the clock on screen. I then read 30 frames a second from every run, about 1,700 per run. On each frame a script finds the cyan target by its colour and measures how far it is from the crosshair at the centre of the screen, left-right and up-down, scaled to the target's size. A frame counts as on target when the crosshair is inside the target's outline. A miss counts when the crosshair is outside for at least 0.1 s. A miss is up-down when the vertical distance is larger, and left-right otherwise. An overshoot is a miss on the opposite side within 0.2 s of the previous one, meaning the crosshair passed through the target. To check it works, time on target matched the scores with a correlation of 0.84. About 4 seconds of each run were left out because the target was too small to detect. (Corrected: I first thought the target was missing. Zeus actually has three rounds of about 20 seconds, and each one starts with the bot far away straight ahead, so it's tiny on screen.) Limits: one session of 12 runs, and the video can't show grip or hand tension.
What happened: I measured the crosshair against the target on every frame. Time on target was 31–38% per run, and it matched the scores closely: the three lowest-scoring runs (453, 455, 472) were the three lowest on target. About 95% of the time off target was left-right misses. Only about 5% came from the up/down kicks, although runs with more kick misses did score lower. Almost half the misses (47%) were overshoots: the crosshair went through the target to the other side right after a correction. Each run had 9–16 long misses of half a second or more, and those decided which runs scored well.
Conditions: The scores show no fatigue. Time on target was the same in the first and last 20 seconds of runs, and the same before and after ZEUS BUILDER.
